# Precedent: BMW Standard Liability Cap Positions

**Status:** Approved precedent  
**Applicable to:** All commercial agreements  
**Jurisdiction:** German law primary; notes on other jurisdictions  
**Last reviewed:** 2024-Q2

---

## BMW Standard Positions

### Tier 1 — Preferred (Lead with this)

> "Each party's aggregate liability to the other, whether arising in contract, tort (including negligence), breach of statutory duty, or otherwise, shall not exceed an amount equal to the total fees or purchase price paid or payable by BMW in the **12 months** immediately preceding the event giving rise to the claim."

### Tier 2 — Acceptable

> "...shall not exceed the greater of (a) EUR [500,000] or (b) the total fees paid in the **24 months** preceding the claim."

### Walk-Away Condition

Any clause imposing unlimited liability on BMW, or any clause limiting BMW's right to recover for supplier defects causing automotive safety issues.

---

## Mandatory Carve-outs (Non-Negotiable)

The liability cap **must not apply** to:

1. Death or personal injury caused by negligence (mandatory under German law — AGB-Recht § 309 Nr. 7 BGB)
2. Fraud or fraudulent misrepresentation
3. Wilful misconduct (Vorsatz) or gross negligence (grobe Fahrlässigkeit)
4. Product liability claims under ProdHaftG (Produkthaftungsgesetz)
5. Data protection breaches where GDPR imposes fines
6. Supplier's indemnification for IP infringement
7. Supplier's obligation to remedy product defects causing recalls

---

## German Law Background

**§ 309 Nr. 7 BGB** (AGB control): Standard terms (AGB) cannot exclude liability for:
- Death or personal injury caused by negligence
- Gross negligence causing property damage

**§ 276 BGB**: Liability for wilful misconduct (Vorsatz) and gross negligence (grobe Fahrlässigkeit) cannot be contractually excluded.

**ProdHaftG (Produkthaftungsgesetz)**: Product liability for defective products cannot be contractually limited against consumers (§ 14 ProdHaftG). Note: BMW's customers are consumers — supplier agreements must preserve BMW's right to recover product liability losses from the supplier.

## Negotiation Notes

**Common counterparty positions and BMW responses:**

| Counterparty Position | BMW Response |
|-----------------------|--------------|
| Cap at EUR 100,000 (too low for BMW scale) | Counter with 12-month fees; explain BMW's exposure to end-customers |
| Mutual cap at 100% of annual contract value | Acceptable only if product defect losses are carved out |
| No cap on either side | Acceptable for contracts under EUR 50,000 total value |
| Cap applies to product recalls | Reject — cannot accept; product safety is existential risk |

**Leverage note:** For critical sole-source suppliers, BMW has accepted 24-month cap. For commodity suppliers, hold firm at 12 months.

## Cross-Jurisdiction Notes

**UK:** Liability caps in B2B contracts are generally enforceable if reasonable (UCTA 1977 reasonableness test). Courts will look at the deal balance and relative bargaining strength. BMW's 12-month cap is generally viewed as reasonable for commercial parties.

**US (varying by state):** Consequential damage waivers are common and generally enforceable. Ensure the cap applies to all damages including indemnification claims.

**France:** Under French law (Art. 1231-3 Code civil), consequential damages not foreseeable at contract formation may be excluded, but liability for faute lourde (gross fault) or dol (fraud) cannot be limited.
